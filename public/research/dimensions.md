# Dimensions: Nether and End travel (batch 7)

Target: Minecraft Java 1.7.10. Not full parity. Evidence is the probe `tools/probes/DimensionProbe.java`, which drives the unchanged obfuscated classes (Teleporter `ahp`, providers `aqo`/`aqp`/`aqq`/`aqr`, `Direction p`, `Entity sa.ah`, `EntityPlayer yz.D`) and writes `public/research/dimensions-reference.json` (103 records: 1 direction, 49 movementDirection, 3 provider, 2 timing, 60 setInPortal, 36 teleport). `src/dimensions.test.js` replays them against `src/dimensions.js`. Run `MC_RESEARCH=<tools/research> node tools/verify-dimensions.mjs` to regenerate.

## What works

- **Save model.** The Overworld is the world record, unchanged: old saves load as before. In the Nether or End the active record is a view (`id = <world>~<dim>`); its block edits use the existing per-column storage under that id. `save()` shelves the root with `dimension` (where the player is) and `dimensions[d] = { state, tiles }`. `state` holds the coordinate-keyed runtime fields (entities, drops, fluid/fire ticks, redstone, spawners, signs, ...; `DIM_KEYS`), `tiles` the chests, furnaces, dispensers, brewing stands and hoppers. Global fields (inventory, health, XP, bed spawn, weather, game rules) are shared. Deleting a world also deletes its `~-1`/`~1` columns. Code: `src/dimension-live.js` (`viewRecord`, `storedRecord`).
- **Nether portal travel** (both ways). `BlockPortal` collision (cells overlapped by the player box shrunk by 0.001) calls `PortalState.setInPortal`; `tick` follows `Entity.onEntityUpdate`: `portalCounter` against `getMaxInPortalTime` (80, or 0 in Creative), `timeUntilPortal` cooldown 10 for the player (300 for other entities), and a decay of 4 per tick outside a portal. Arrival: `transferTarget` (x/z divided or multiplied by 8, clamped) and the ported `Teleporter` (`placeInExistingPortal`, a 128-block search with the per-column cache, then `makePortal`). The Teleporter runs on a read-only terrain peek plus the target dimension's saved edits; its writes are applied after the columns load. Replayed against 36 probe teleports (positions, motion, yaw, writes).
- **End portal travel.** The end portal block (internal 149) sends the player to the End at the entrance (100, 50, 0): `Teleporter.placeInPortal` for dimension 1 builds the 5 x 5 obsidian platform with 3 layers of air and places the player at (100, 49, 0). Entering again from a bed-less Overworld uses the same code. The exit portal (the same block inside the End) shows the original End poem and credits, then returns to the bed spawn if the bed is valid or to the world spawn. Inventory is kept. The End is a real streamed world (`DimensionWorld`) with entities, tiles and edits saved. First entry calls `DimensionLive.onDragonSpawn({x:0,y:128,z:0})` once (`dragonSpawned` is saved); the dragon track assigns that hook (default: no-op).
- **Death in a dimension.** `canRespawnHere` is false in both: the respawn button first travels to the Overworld (state kept, no Teleporter) and then runs the existing respawn (bed or world spawn).
- **Rendering and rules.** Nether: no sky, sun, stars or clouds; fog (0.2, 0.03, 0.03); no sky light (`hasNoSky`: `LightVolume` heights = height, no inflow); light table with 0.1 ambient (rewrites the shared `BRIGHTNESS` array); celestial angle fixed 0.5; no weather. End: `environment/end_sky.png` on a six-face cube (radius 100, UV 0..16, colour 0x282828, `RenderGlobal.renderSky` rotations), no sun/moon/stars/clouds, fog from `endFogColor`, angle fixed 0, no weather. Water buckets in the Nether fizz (`random.fizz`, 8 `largesmoke`) and place nothing (`ItemBucket.a(ahb,III)Z` reads `ahb.t.f`, hasNoSky: Nether only). Beds explode in the Nether and End (power 5, flaming, smoking). The clock spins outside the Overworld. Portal overlay (`portal.png`, first frame) with the alpha of `renderPortalOverlay`, the existing nausea warp, `portal.trigger` on entering a portal and `portal.travel` on leaving.
- **Terrain.** Nether: `ChunkProviderHell` terrain (batch 6) plus population (see approximations). End: `EndTerrain` plus `populateEndChunk`. Both stream in the 8 x 8 window.
- **Spawning.** The 1.7.10 Hell list selects ghasts (50), zombie pigmen (100) and magma cubes (1). Inside a fortress, the bridge supplies its list, including blazes and skeletons (80% wither skeletons). End packs use Endermen. The SpawnerAnimals port ([natural spawning](natural-spawning.md)) runs in the ticking window and respects Peaceful and `doMobSpawning`. Fortress chests and Blaze spawner records are handed to the live world once per generated column.
- **End lightmap.** The 1.7.10 client `EntityRenderer.updateLightmap` (`blt.i(float)`) replaces the sky contribution with RGB base `[0.22, 0.28, 0.25]` plus `0.75` times the per-channel block-light curves, before Night Vision and brightness adjustment. The original `GuiWinGame` (`beg`) supplies the poem and credits from the client assets. The browser rolls them at 20 CSS pixels per second and offers Escape or a Return to world button.
- **Test helper.** `window.__mc.travel(dim)` and `window.__mc.dims`. No commands were added.

## Approximations

- Nether and End population is per column and stateless: each column runs the populate step of its own chunk and of the -x/-z neighbours, each on a `Random` seeded from that chunk (the original uses one carried-over `Random` and populates once after the +x/+z neighbours exist). Terrain is exact; fortress, lava lakes, fire, glowstone and mushroom placement differ from the original. Fire's neighbour update is not ported in the generation world, so those populate steps are skipped (a warning is logged once).
- The Teleporter's search reads terrain only (no population, no structures) of the target dimension plus saved edits; features that population adds later can overlap a new portal.
- Overworld arrival uses the streamed generator's terrain without trees and structures.
- Yaw: the game's yaw is converted with `pi - mcYaw`.

## Browser and Java saves

Browser backups include each dimension's edits and runtime in `dimensions[d]`. The Java ZIP places Anvil region files under `region/`, `DIM-1/region/` and `DIM1/region/`; `level.dat` records the player's current dimension and position. The ZIP still includes a browser backup.

The portal timer belongs to the player across dimension changes. Browser saves and backups preserve both the partial entry counter and the cooldown. Reopening a legacy save starts both at zero. Java 1.7.10 `Entity` (`sa`) writes only the cooldown as the integer `PortalCooldown`, which the Anvil export includes in `level.dat`.

The compass uses the 32 original frames in the hotbar, inventory, Creative Search tab and 3D item renderers. It points toward the Overworld spawn, including a spawn changed with `/setworldspawn`, and spins in the Nether and End. The browser saves the spawn independently of the player's position, and the Anvil export writes it in native coordinates. An 80-tick trace of the unchanged `TextureCompass` (`bqm.a(ahb,DDDZZ)`) checks a moving and turning player. The Nether and End random-angle branch follows the original bytecode.

The existing region converter accepts paths under the native dimension directories. For example, to convert two selected 1 × 1 chunk areas at native chunk coordinates (-1, -1):

```sh
node tools/import-java-region.mjs --seed 17 \
  --nether-area -1,-1,1,1 --end-area -1,-1,1,1 \
  --out example.blockworld.json DIM-1/region/r.-1.-1.mca DIM1/region/r.-1.-1.mca
```

Import the resulting JSON through Select World. The converter keeps native coordinates in the dimensions and supports its usual selected areas up to 8 × 8 chunks each. It doesn't read player data from a native `level.dat`, copy fortress start NBT, or retain every entity and tile field. The conversion report lists omissions. Existing ZIP export limits apply across all dimensions.

## Batch 29: the Overworld while the player is away, saplings and leaves, import

- **What natively runs (bytecode-read, `ms` = ChunkProviderServer, `mt` = WorldServer, `MinecraftServer.updateTimeLightAndEntities`).** The server loop ticks every `WorldServer` each tick (`mt.b()` then `mt.h()`), with no player test. In `mt.b()` `ahb.o` (updateWeather) runs first, then the world time and total time increment (the Overworld only: `DerivedWorldInfo` of the Nether/End ignores the writes), then the scheduled ticks, the chunk loop over the active set, villages and block events. The active set is built from the world's own players, so with the player elsewhere the Overworld takes no random ticks and no mob spawning. Scheduled ticks and entity updates continue in the chunks that stay loaded. `ms.b(x, z)` (unloadChunksIfNotNearSpawn) queues an unused chunk for unloading except where `aqo.e()` (canRespawnHere, true only in the Overworld) and the chunk centre is within 128 blocks of the spawn point: the Overworld keeps a spawn area, the Nether and End unload everything.
- **Ported.** World time already continued (it is the player's `time`, saved in the shared root; the browser run below shows 20 ticks per second with no double advance across the two trips). New: `Weather.awayOverworld` keeps a second Weather holding the Overworld's rain/thunder timers, flags, strengths and its `worldRandom` stream, advanced by `updateWeather` each tick in `tickDimension`; `saveWeather()` and `saveOverworldSimulation()` write them to the root record (the view's `weather` and `worldRandom` fields reach the root through `storedRecord`). A save made while away also keeps the live `rain` and `thunder` strengths (browser extension; the native NBT has only the four fields and restarts at full strength).
- **Not ported.** The Overworld's spawn-area scheduled ticks (water, lava, fire, redstone, pistons) and entity updates (items, mobs, carts, TNT) stay frozen while the player is away; the chunks are saved and resume on return. The Overworld's `worldRandom` draws only for the weather timers while away (natively also the spawn-area chunk loop is idle, so the stream there is only the timers). The travel loading screens pause the game tick for about 4 s per trip, where the integrated server keeps ticking.
- **Saplings and leaves (`dimension-tree-reference.json`, `DimTreeProbe.java`, `dimension-trees.test.js`).** Bytecode-read: `BlockSapling` reads only `ahb.k` (block light value), `BlockLeaves.updateTick` only `ahb.b` (checkChunksExist), `WorldGenTrees` a literal 256 height (not the provider's actual height), no provider call. On the unchanged classes over real Nether and End chunks: oak saplings on dirt with the cell above at light 0, 8, 9, 10 and 14 (4 sites, 24 seeds each, up to 60 ticks), and a leaf cube around a log; the port's Trees.tickSapling (marks, normal and big growth, restore after a failed growth next to a glowstone) and tickLeaves give every event string, the cells changed (sha256) and the next Random int. No port change was needed. Leaves that would decay and drop items were not probed (the harness has no GameRules or world Random).
- **Import.** `world-import.js` now lists `simulation` among the runtime fields and checks both `worldRandom` and `simulation` as valid Random snapshots; `importedDimensionState` runs on each imported `dimensions[d]` record in main.js. The `DIM_KEYS` of the region and whole-save importers already included `simulation`.

## Batch 30: the Overworld's spawn area while the player is away (`away-world.js`)

Evidence type: bytecode-read (javap on the 1.7.10 jar; `mt` = WorldServer, `ms` = ChunkProviderServer, `ahb` = World, `wb` = VillageSiege, `wc` = VillageCollection). No probe was run, so the unload rule and the 1200-tick counter are reads, not recorded behaviour.

- **Chunks.** `ms.b(int, int)` (unloadChunksIfNotNearSpawn) applies only when the provider `canRespawnHere` (the Overworld). A chunk whose centre (16c + 8) is within +-128 blocks of the world spawn (`mt.K()`) is never queued for unload; every other chunk is queued and `ms.a` unloads up to 100 per tick. So with nobody in the Overworld about 16 x 16 chunks around the spawn stay loaded and the rest unload. The Nether and the End unload everything (batch 29). That the PlayerManager unwatch path queues the chunks when the player leaves is assumed, not read.
- **Scheduled block updates.** `mt.b()` calls `tickUpdates` (`a(Z)Z`, at most 1000 entries a tick) with no player test: water, lava, fire, falling blocks, repeaters, torches, comparators and the redstone queue keep running in loaded chunks for as long as the player is away.
- **Entities and tile entities.** `mt.h()` (updateEntities): with an empty `ahb.h` (player list) a counter `P` is tested as `P++ >= 1200` and the method returns before `ahb.h()`; `mt.i()` resets `P` to 0 while a player is present. Entities and tile entities (furnaces, hoppers, brewing stands, piston tiles) therefore update for 1200 ticks (60 s) after the last player leaves and then freeze. Scheduled updates are not behind this counter.
- **Random ticks, spawning, villages.** The active chunk set (`mt.g()`) is built from the world's own players, the natural spawner (`aho.a`) iterates the world's players, and `wb.a()` (siege) picks a village near a player: none of them run with no player in the world. `wc.a()` (village tick) and the weather and daylight advance in `mt.b()` still run (weather: batch 29).
- **Ported.** On leaving the Overworld, `DimensionLive.beginAway` copies the live blocks of the spawn-area columns that hold work (scheduled fluid/fire/falling ticks, falling entities, redstone queue, torches, pistons, furnaces, hoppers, brewing stands) plus a one-column halo, reads their stored edits and starts `away-worker.js`. On return the worker runs `(physics ticks away) + (loading-screen ms / 50)` ticks (cap 72000, 4 s wall budget) over a headless Superflat shell World built from those copies, with `Fluids`, `Fire`, `FallingBlocks`, the redstone runtime, `Hoppers`, brewing and the furnaces. Per tick: scheduled updates and the redstone queue always, tile entities, falling entities and piston tiles only for the first 1200 ticks; after that it stops as soon as every queue is empty. The result patches the saved root (runtime ticks, redstone, pistons, tile maps, washed-away/falling drops) and the edited columns are written to the chunk store before the Overworld opens. `away-world.test.js` covers the spawn-area predicate, the column selection, the 1200-tick gate, a water tick due after the gate and the DimensionLive wiring.
- **Loading-screen ticks.** `tickCounter` pauses while a trip loads, so `DimensionLive.travel` times each `openWorld` and the Overworld clock (`survival.time`) is advanced by `ms / 50` after a trip (not while `doDaylightCycle` is off); the same ms count as away ticks for the run. Weather timers are not advanced by this.
- **Not ported (approximations).** Mobs, items, projectiles, minecarts, boats and primed TNT do not move while away (the saved entity lists are untouched, not frozen at 1200 ticks like the native ones); dispensers and droppers keep their contents and do not fire; command blocks, note blocks and jukeboxes do not run; tile entities outside the departure's container window (the resident columns plus margin) are not in memory and are not advanced; spawn-area columns outside the 8 x 8 resident window at departure are not loaded in the run, so work there waits (as a non-ticking column does); imported whole saves and legacy finite worlds are skipped; the run uses its own fluid random stream, not the shared `World.rand`; weather does not touch fire or rain during the run; a falling block spawned after the gate gets one entity tick before it freezes; worlds with a worker failure or timeout simply resume frozen, as before.

## Batch 31: entities, dispensers and weather in the away run (`away-entities.js`)

Evidence type: bytecode-read again (javap on `mt`, WorldServer), no probe. `mt.h()` (updateEntities): `if (ahb.h.isEmpty()) { if (P++ >= 1200) return; } else i();` then `ahb.h()`. The test uses the old value of `P`, so exactly 1200 ticks run with no player (P = 0..1199); `mt.i()` (`P = 0`) is called only from `mt.h` itself, while the player list is non-empty. Loading-screen ticks count, since the server keeps ticking.

- **Entities in the run.** For the first 1200 ticks the run ticks the live `ItemEntities` (fall, drag, merge, 6000-tick despawn age), `ExperienceOrbs`, primed TNT (`tickPrimedTnt`, the explosion through `applyExplosion` with no mobs, arrows or projectiles), `Minecarts` (empty, chest, furnace and TNT carts on rails, with detector-rail hooks) and `Boats`, on the headless away world; falling blocks already ran. Drops and spills from the fluid wash, falling blocks, explosions (block drops, tile contents, orbs) and carts are item entities during the gate and saved records after it. Redstone entity detection (plates, tripwire, hoppers picking up items, pistons) now sees the run's items, orbs, TNT, carts and boats.
- **What is saved.** The result carries the whole saved lists (`drops`, `xpOrbs`, `tnt`, `minecarts`, `boats`): records outside the loaded spawn columns, vehicles with a rider or a mob passenger (`riding`), and hopper, spawner and command carts stay as saved; the run's entities are serialized in their save format. Columns holding saved entities now count as work (`stateColumns`).
- **Dispensers and droppers** fire (they are scheduled block ticks) through the entity systems above: item spawn, TNT, carts, buckets, flint and steel. A dispense that needs a projectile system (arrow, egg, snowball, potion, fire charge, firework) or bone meal on a non-crop is not run: the stack is put back. Command blocks are skipped (no command interpreter; before this the run would have thrown on one). Note blocks and jukeboxes run their scheduled ticks and make no sound.
- **Weather.** `Weather.advance(n)` rolls `updateWeather` for the loading-screen ticks, on the Overworld's away copy while in the Nether/End. `DimensionLive.travel` now reports the loading ticks for every trip (the clock was only advanced on the return trip before).
- **Still not ported.** Mobs (no movement, gravity or despawn while away, and they are not hurt by explosions), projectiles in flight, firework rockets, hanging entities, entities in columns outside the resident window at departure, and mob riders. Wall-clock budget stops the entity run early on a very busy spawn area (`stats.stopped = "budget"`).

### Batch 33: command blocks, ender pearls and block light while away

- Command blocks run in the away run (`src/away-commands.js`) as scheduled block ticks, like the original `BlockCommandBlock.updateTick`, which has no player test (bytecode-read with javap, not executed). The commands run through the same `createCommandHost` the live game uses, on the run's own world, weather and entity systems. SuccessCount and LastOutput are saved back. Command minecarts (type 6) run their command on the cart's activator-rail tick.
- Selector rule (bytecode-read): `CommandHandler.a` runs a command once per player `PlayerSelector.matchPlayers` returns; no match means no run, no message, SuccessCount 0 and LastOutput unchanged. The sender's world is only passed to the player search when `rm`, `r`, `x`, `y` or `z` is given, so a plain `@p` or `@a` still finds the player in the Nether or End, while `@p[r=..]` and `@p[x=..]` find nobody. The run returns the silent zero for the second kind. Commands that would act on the player (give, tp, xp, gamemode, effect, clear, kill, enchant, spawnpoint, difficulty, tell/tellraw to the player, scoreboard, achievement) are skipped: SuccessCount and LastOutput stay as they were and `stats.commands.skipped` counts them. Skips are not native behaviour, because the worker has no player state.
- Time, weather, gamerules, defaultgamemode and `say`/`me` output from commands are applied to the live objects on return (`awayResult`), not written into the saved root, because the save after the run would overwrite them. Chat is replayed on return, capped at 100 lines.
- Ender pearls in flight tick. `EntityEnderPearl.onImpact` (bytecode-read): a hit entity takes 0 thrown damage, portal particles spawn, and the thrower teleports only if it is an `EntityPlayerMP` in the same world. With the player away the pearl simply dies, so the run lands pearls and removes them.
- Block light: away mobs read block light from a flooded volume of the held columns (`src/away-light.js`, the same fixed point as `LightVolume` and `RingLight`), instead of open sky only. It is lazy (free with no emitter) and re-floods at most every 10 ticks or twice the last flood time. A full flood of 64 columns with 40 torches measured 40-50 ms in Node; the browser check logged 2 floods, 66 ms total, inside a 300 ms run.
- Leashed mobs whose knot is on a fence in a ticked column now tick.
- Still not ported: commands that act on the player are skipped, not queued; worldspawn changes do not move the run's area; sky light is open-sky only; lit furnaces do not emit light; pearl-hit knockback uses the pearl position; command blocks alone do not make a column count as active. The live port prints "Player not found" for a no-match selector at the username index where the original is silent (outside the away code). The 1200 counter is bytecode-read only.

### Batch 34: spawning, player commands, the shared tick tree and furnace light while away

- Natural spawning and spawners (bytecode-read with javap, not executed): `SpawnerAnimals.findChunksForSpawning` (`aho.a`) is called from `WorldServer.tick` (`mt.b`) and builds its chunk set from `World.playerEntities` (`ahb.h`, the same list as `mt.h`), which holds only players in that world. With the player in the Nether or End the Overworld list is empty, so the chunk set is empty and nothing spawns. The same list drives `MobSpawnerBaseLogic.func_98279_f` (`agq.f`): its `getEntityWorld().getClosestPlayer(x+.5, y+.5, z+.5, requiredPlayerRange)` (`agq.g`, range 16 by default) returns null, so a mob spawner does not tick its delay or spawn. The away run therefore adds no natural or spawner spawning, which is the native result; `src/away-spawn.test.js` pins it (a dark, spawner-lit column spawns nothing over 1200+ ticks; only existing mobs, summons and egg-like command spawns exist).
- Player-acting commands are queued, not skipped. A command block or command cart whose command acts on the player (give, clear, effect, enchant, gamemode, xp, tell, tellraw, achievement, and a literal player name or `@p/@a/@r` the native code resolves to the away player) goes into an ordered list (cap 2000) returned with the run. On return `replayPlayerCommands` runs the list in firing order through the live command host, with the sender's native text, and writes the block's SuccessCount and LastOutput (and the saved cart record's). Timestamps use the plan's wall clock plus 50 ms per tick, so they are estimates. The replay runs in `awayResult`, before `openWorld` builds the Overworld record, and the player state it changes (inventory, experience, mode, achievements, stats) is copied into the root the return uses.

### Batch 36: the player's trace while away, `/setworldspawn` mid-run, per-write fluid notification, which entities tick

All of this is bytecode-read (javap on the 1.7.10 jar), not executed. Rules not re-checked this batch are in the batch 35 section above.

- Player state at each firing. The main thread (`dimension-live.js` `traceAway`, called from `tick` while an away run is pending) records a trace of the trip: a `[t, dimension, x, y, z]` sample about every 20 ticks and, only when the serialized player state (inventory, experience, game mode, health...) differs from the last snapshot, a `[t, snapshot]` entry (bounds: 4000 samples, 300 snapshots; the last snapshot is replaced when full). `t` counts as the run's ticks do (game ticks plus the loading screens' ms / 50). The worker's shadow player (`away-commands.js` `syncShadow`) starts from the departure snapshot and, at each firing, moves to the position sample in force (at most about a second old) and applies what the player changed between consecutive snapshots (his own pickups, drops, crafts: per (id, damage) count difference; experience gained is added, a drop in total takes the snapshot's experience; game mode when it changed) on top of the effects of earlier commands. Health, hunger and effects are not carried. Without a trace (no `host.awayPlayer`) the return state serves for the whole run, as in batch 35.
- Consequence for the return: the live player already has everything he picked up, so re-running `give` and `clear` on the return state is wrong (a `clear` that found nothing at tick 20 would take what he found at tick 400). The run now returns `inventoryDelta`, the shadow's inventory against the last state the player was seen in (the last snapshot), and `replayPlayerCommands` applies that as one net change for the entries the shadow ran (`inv`). The other queued commands (`xp`, `gamemode`, `effect`, `enchant`, `tell`, `tellraw`, `achievement`) still replay on the live player; the fire-time SuccessCount and LastOutput are kept (the replay no longer rewrites them for shadow-run entries).
- `/spawnpoint` (`CommandSetSpawnpoint` `bp.b`): with no argument a command block fails natively (`CommandBase.b(sender)` `y.b` throws "You must specify which player you wish to perform this action on." unless the sender is an `EntityPlayerMP`), which the port already did. With a target (`@p`, a name) and no coordinates the target's own block position is used, `EntityPlayerMP.f_`: `floor(posX)`, `floor(posY + 0.5)`, `floor(posZ)`, then `setSpawnChunk(pos, true)` (forced, the Overworld respawn point whatever his dimension). Both the live `src/commands.js` (it read the SENDER's position and `floor(y)`: wrong for a command block and for half-block heights) and the away run now read the target's position with the +0.5 rounding; the away run takes it from the trace at the firing tick and writes `playerSpawn` at once (it is not queued: the live player is elsewhere at return). Without a trace the form is still skipped.
- `/setworldspawn` mid-run. What keeps the spawn chunks loaded is `ChunkProviderServer.b(II)` (`ms.b`, unloadChunksIfNotNearSpawn): it queues a chunk for unload unless the chunk's centre is within 128 blocks of `WorldServer.K()` (the spawn read at call time) in a dimension where `canRespawnHere` holds. It is called when the last watcher leaves a chunk (`PlayerInstance` removal, `mr.b(mw)`) and from `WorldServer.saveAllChunks` (`mt.a(boolean, qk)`) for every loaded chunk the player manager does not watch. `MinecraftServer.tick` runs the save when `tickCounter % 900 == 0` (`sipush 900; irem`). Nothing loads the chunks a moved spawn would newly cover (the initial 25 x 25 load is at startup only). So a `/setworldspawn` during the run does not extend the area; the next autosave unloads every chunk outside the new area (the unload itself runs on the next tick, up to 100 per tick, which the port treats as immediate), and the loaded set is the intersection of every spawn area it was checked against. The port: `away-world.js` keeps a list of spawn areas (`areas`), `plan.tickBase` (the game tick counter at departure, standing in for `tickCounter`) gives the autosave phase, and block writes, tile entities, command `setblock`/`summon` and entities all read the current loaded set. The new spawn is still returned and applies to the next trip. Not ported: scheduled ticks sitting in a column that unloads mid-run run on (their writes are refused) instead of being re-queued each tick as `WorldServer.tickUpdates` does for entries whose neighbourhood is not loaded.
- Which entities tick. `WorldServer.updateEntities` (`ahb.h`) calls `World.updateEntity` (`ahb.g`) = `updateEntityWithOptionalForce(entity, true)` (`ahb.a(sa, boolean)`), which returns at once unless `checkChunksExist(x - 32, 0, z - 32, x + 32, 0, z + 32)` (`ahb.b(IIIIII)`: every chunk in the box passes `chunkExists`) holds. So an entity only ticks when every chunk within 32 blocks of it is loaded: with the spawn area's chunk centres within +-128 blocks, entities within 32 blocks of its edge (about x or z beyond +-95 of the spawn) do not tick at all while the player is away, the inner part does (for 1200 ticks, then the idle gate). Tile entities only need their own chunk (`blockExists`). The port had tested only the entity's own column; `away-world.js` `entityTicks` now applies the 32-block rule at plan time (every entity list) and each tick for mobs (`mobs.isActive`, which fireballs and skulls read) and hanging entities. Items, orbs, TNT, carts, boats, arrows and thrown projectiles are tested only at plan time: one that moves across the 32-block line during the run is not frozen at it.
- Fluid notification. The away world now notifies redstone per fluid write through `fluids.writers` and flushes after each fluid run, as `main.js` does (batch 35 ticktree3), instead of passing the whole run's changes after it.

### Batch 37: health, hunger and effects in the shadow player, held scheduled ticks, the 32-block rule per tick, the autosave phase

Bytecode-read (javap on the 1.7.10 jar) unless stated; nothing here was executed against the native classes.

- Which commands change health, hunger or effects. `CommandEffect` (`am`) calls only `EntityLivingBase.addPotionEffect` (`yz.c`), `removePotionEffect` (`yz.m`) and `clearActivePotions` (`yz.aP`) on the selected player, whatever dimension he is in. Every other player command an away run can reach (`give`, `clear`, `xp`, `gamemode`, `enchant`, `spawnpoint`, `achievement`, `tell`, `tellraw`) leaves health, the food stats and effects alone. So `/effect` is the only command whose change to those fields has to be carried; `@p` selectors in 1.7.10 have no health or hunger argument (only level `l`/`lm`, game mode `m`, position, count, team, name and score), and level and mode were already in the shadow.
- The trace now carries the body. Each position sample of `traceAway` has a sixth item `[health, food, saturation, exhaustion, effects]` (the effects as `{id, duration, amplifier}`). The state snapshots no longer change when only those numbers, the air, the timers or an effect's duration move (they changed on nearly every sample while walking and would have filled the 300-snapshot cap in five minutes); a snapshot is written when inventory, experience or game mode changed, as before. `away-commands.js` `syncShadow` follows the player's own change between two samples as a difference (health, food, saturation, exhaustion; an effect he gained, lost or refreshed, told from the count-down he would have anyway) on top of what the commands did to the shadow. The effects the commands gave tick on the shadow (`Effects.tick`: regeneration, poison, wither, hunger, saturation per tick; the invulnerability frames count down); the player's own only count down, because what they did to his health is already in the sample differences. Instant health and damage are applied at the firing by the command host (natively `Potion.performEffect` on the next tick, the same tick for this purpose).
- The return. The run returns `playerDelta`: the net health, food, saturation and exhaustion the effect commands changed, the effects they left on him with the time they have left, and the ones they took away that are still gone at the end. `replayPlayerCommands` applies it once (`applyPlayerDelta`: effects first, then the numbers; a lethal total goes through `Survival.damage("magic")`) and does not re-run the `effect` commands, so a `/effect @p regeneration 60` fired at tick 20 no longer gives 60 s at the return but what is left of it, and a removal that found something at tick 700 stays removed. Entries without a delta (no trace) still replay. `/xp` is not a delta: it still replays on the live player (the shadow already shows its level to later `@p[l=..]` and `xp` commands).
- Not carried: `Absorption` amount changes by commands beyond the effect add/remove, a health change that would kill the shadow before the return (the shadow stops healing and poisoning at 0 and the delta carries the loss; the live player then dies on return through `Survival.damage`, not at the firing), and anything drawn from a position sample finer than about a second.
- Scheduled ticks in a column that leaves the loaded set. `WorldServer.tickUpdates` ticks an entry only when `checkChunksExist` radius 0 holds (see `tile-ticks.js`, batch 33): in an unloaded chunk it is dropped from memory, but the chunk's save (`AnvilChunkLoader`, `TileTicks`, `t = scheduledTime - worldTime`) holds it and a later load re-queues it with the same remaining delay. The port already held fluid, fire and falling-block entries back in unloaded columns (their `beginTick` puts the time back, so the saved delay is the one they had when the column left the area) but ran the redstone list's entries against refused writes (a repeater or torch tick in a column the autosave unloaded was consumed, the run's repeater state lost). `away-world.js` now puts the time of every redstone entry in a non-loaded column back each tick (`freezeUnloadedRedstone`), so it is saved with its remaining delay; entries never loaded (a column the run did not copy) are held from the start, which is the saved-chunk case. `idle()` ignores held entries, so a run whose only work is held entries ends as idle after the entity gate. Not ported: a scheduler call from a loaded column into an unloaded one (`World.scheduleBlockUpdate` also needs `checkChunksExist`, so natively nothing is queued there; the port queues it and holds it), and the unload rate (100 chunks per tick, treated as immediate).
- The 32-block rule each tick. `World.updateEntities` asks `updateEntityWithOptionalForce(e, true)` for every loaded entity every tick, so an entity that flies, rolls, floats or falls to within 32 blocks of a chunk that is not loaded stops ticking where it is (position, motion and age kept). `away-entities.js` `park` takes the entities that fail `entityTicks` out of a system's list for the length of that system's tick (items, experience orbs, primed TNT, carts, boats, arrows, thrown projectiles and potions, rockets; falling blocks in `away-world.js`) and puts them back; mobs, fireballs and hanging entities already read the rule per tick. `entityTicks` skips the chunk loop for positions at least 80 blocks inside every loaded area. The count of entity-ticks held back is `stats.entities.held`.
- The autosave phase (`MinecraftServer.tickCounter % 900 == 0`). The port cannot know the server's `tickCounter`: natively it counts the integrated server's ticks since the world was opened (it restarts when the player leaves to the title screen and opens a world), while `main.js` `tickCounter` counts game ticks since the page loaded (not checked here whether it stops while paused, as the single-player server does) and is not reset when another world is opened in the same page. `plan.tickBase` is that page counter at departure, `ran` counts the run's own ticks (game ticks plus the loading screens' ms / 50), so the phase is right for a first world opened right after page load and is a fixed but arbitrary phase otherwise. Natively the phase is equally arbitrary from the player's side (it depends on how long the session has been running), so this is a documented approximation, not a rule: resetting it per world would need the world-open event in `main.js` and was not done.
